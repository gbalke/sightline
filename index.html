GPS now—

Auto uses GPS altitude only while the phone reports a vertical accuracy within the limit above and the reading doesn't put you underground; otherwise it uses ground height plus eye height. Eye height is ignored when GPS is used. GPS altitude on Android may be measured from the ellipsoid rather than sea level, which differs by tens of metres in some places: stand at ground level outdoors once and press the zero button to measure and remove that offset.

Turning is always measured by the gyro. The compass only sets north, slowly. Magnetic cases, mounts and car dashboards distort it; calibrating on a landmark you can identify on the map fixes north, and with the option above the compass is then ignored until you clear calibration. Recalibrate if the heading creeps after several minutes.

View

If overlay streets look stretched or squeezed relative to the real ones, adjust FOV. If they are shifted sideways or up/down, calibrate on a landmark. Contours are drawn from the same elevation data as the ray, every fifth line thicker and labelled; anything hidden behind terrain is left out. Buildings and trees are not in the elevation data, so they don't hide anything. Street data © OpenStreetMap contributors.
